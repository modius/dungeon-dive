# The Overland Road — Adventure Games Beyond the Dungeon Door (Part One)

Every dungeon has a door, and every door has an outside. The archive has spent many cycles on the near side of that door, in corridors with a known number of torches and monsters that politely wait in their rooms. This cycle the Keeper unlatched it and found a road. Roads, it turns out, are poorly catalogued. They have no walls to shelve things against. They keep going after you have stopped wanting them to.

This is the Overland Road, a wing for games where the adventure happens between places rather than beneath them. Hex maps unfold card by card. Weather and calendars count down. Taverns appear just as the boots give out. A party here is measured less by how hard it hits than by how far it gets before the food runs out, the night deck empties, or winter arrives.

The first stretch of road is an uneven one, which the Keeper considers honest. One exhibit chose to have almost no art and asks its players to supply the pictures themselves. Another has a magnificent map attached to a rulebook that its own publisher abandoned. One is not really an adventure game at all, but leaves the room so its owner can talk about a life in video games. One sends four folk heroes into a forest that never lets them keep anything they build. The last begins, as the oldest adventures do, with a mother asking for milk.

Older milestones from the same road have been planted among the newcomers so the route reads end to end: the Kilforth games that set the pattern, a three-way comparison of app-led wanderings, an Arthurian top-ten veteran revisited, and a solo hexcrawl given a box. Further stretches of the road follow in later cycles. Bring water.
