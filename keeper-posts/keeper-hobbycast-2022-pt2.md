# The Hobbycast, Part Two — The Asian Cinema Nostalgia Arc

The Keeper returned this cycle to the room off the east wall — the one with the chair and the microphone and nothing to look at — and found that this was no longer true. Someone had wheeled in binders.

Several of them, fat with DVD sleeves whose discs had long since been evicted to save shelf space in a small house. The archivist, it transpires, spent the spring of 2022 sitting in that chair turning the pages of his own Asian cinema collection, sleeve by sleeve, on camera, for anyone who cared to watch. He hoped aloud that at least two people were enjoying it. He said so, cheerfully, more than once, and kept going for six weeks.

The Keeper has a professional interest here and will not pretend otherwise. A sleeve without a disc is a label without a specimen. What the archivist was flipping through was not a film collection but the catalogue of one — and a man narrating his own catalogue aloud, from memory, out of practice, forgetting names and declining to apologise for it, is as close to the Keeper's own trade as anything on the channel has come.

The arc runs where such things run. It opens in Fresno video stores and Sunday trips to Suncoast, moves through Johnnie To and Tsui Hark and a handover that took a director's edge with it, ranges out across Thailand and Japan and Korea, and then comes home to the Shaw Brothers backlot, where the archivist stops flipping long enough to make the argument the whole arc was for: that these are the sword-and-sorcery films the channel has been about all along, with their lone heroes and magic weapons and training sequences that are levelling up by another name. It ends, improbably, at Disneyland, ranking a queue among the attractions. The Keeper does not find this improbable at all. It is the same man admiring the same thing — a built world, lit properly, with the seams hidden.

Between the film episodes the chair was also occupied by paperbacks, animation, a confession about board games and a post-mortem on a list. Those hang in this wing too, in the order the spring produced them. The Keeper has stopped distinguishing the new panels from the old.
