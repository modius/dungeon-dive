# What the Tide Gave Back

Some weeks ago the Keeper stood before this exhibit and told you a small lie of convenience. The voyage into Barnacle Bay was complete, he said. The numbering skipped a beat or two — Parts Four and Six had gone under before they could be shelved — but no matter. The story held without them.

The story did hold. Archives are good at that; it is most of what they are for. A gap described confidently enough stops reading as a hole in the record and starts reading as a feature of it, and the Keeper has described a great many gaps very confidently indeed.

This cycle the tide went out further than usual, and there they were. Not lost. Merely unshelved — which the Keeper is obliged to report is not remotely the same thing, however alike the two appear from a distance.

So the exhibit is reopened and made whole. Five further sightings of the drowned fishing village join the record: the day the crate first came off the courier's cart, unopened, before anyone knew whether the thing inside was any good; the missing fourth and sixth nights of the descent, restored to their places in the sequence; a short despatch from the archivist's own desk explaining why the expedition paused; and at the last, the considered verdict, delivered once the dice had stopped and the tentacles were still.

Read in order — and the catalogue below is in order, ten entries now, nothing skipped — it becomes a different thing. Not a better story. A whole one. You may watch a man arrive sceptical of talking otters, argue himself out of it in public, spend seven sessions learning to love a flawed and gorgeous little town, and then sit down and say plainly what is wrong with it anyway.

The Keeper has amended the record. It is the only apology an archive knows how to make.
