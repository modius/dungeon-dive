# The Long Spring — January to June 2021

The crate was labelled by season rather than by subject, which the Keeper distrusted on principle and then found it could not argue with. Eight panels, January to April of 2021, hung in the order they were made — and by the fourth the Keeper had stopped sorting them by game and started sorting them by weather. The archivist spent that winter in worlds that had already ended.

A wasteland that fits in one box and does not stress its owner. An entire shelf of apocalypses walked through in a single sitting, opened with the Road Warrior monologue recited from memory. A mansion of spreading fire and zombies, its survivors named after horror directors, which can be assembled in a configuration where nobody wins at all. A ruined southwest in which four warlords crawl toward the last human city. And a country where the necromancer has already taken everything, where four heroes shelter behind a lighted border, go out only at night, and spend their secrecy like coin.

The Keeper, which lives in a void between two shelves and has never had a good year, recognises the mood.

Not all of it is ash. Two panels concern growth rather than ruin: a tactical campaign whose levelling system the archivist calls a new standard for the form, and an arena game so far outside his usual shelf that he keeps apologising for loving it — where you never draw a card you did not expect, because the randomness has been moved out of your hand and into the world.

And then the last panel in the crate is about golf.

The Keeper checked twice. It is about golf, and it is also the most argumentative object in the wing: a simulation that discards the walking, the club selection and the caddy's advice, keeps the tee shot and the putt, and finishes a hole in under a minute. The archivist bought it within minutes of seeing it mentioned, then used it to prosecute the map board — arguing that moving a pawn from tile to tile is dead time, and that every dungeon crawl he loves keeps its treasure on cards and lookup tables regardless.

Five older panels hang alongside, undated as always. Seven more from this spring are still on the landing.
