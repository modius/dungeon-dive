# Borrowed Fiction — Page, Screen, and Panel on the Table

A library holds two kinds of book: the ones it owns and the ones it has borrowed. The second kind is the more troublesome, because sooner or later someone comes to collect. This cycle the Keeper opened the annex where the borrowed stories live. It is a long, crowded room, and every shelf in it belongs, strictly speaking, to someone else.

Here the stories of other people's books, films and comic panels have been pressed into cardboard and wooden pieces, with the edges sanded down until they fit a box lid. An outlaw of Sherwood is now a wooden meeple. A She-Devil with a Sword takes orders from four dice. A whole Arabian night has been parcelled into numbered paragraphs, a Musketeer has been fitted with a facing token, and a castle once explored by mouse-click has become a deck of cards. None of the original owners was consulted. The Keeper suspects several would have objections. At least one, a cryptid with a grudge against bridges, would probably have objections to everything.

What the annex teaches is that a borrowed story is judged twice: once against the game it became, and once against the book it came from. Some exhibits pass the first test and fail the second. Some are too faithful to their source to please anyone who doesn't already love it. A few come with their own reading lists, as if apologising to the author. And one, the Keeper notes approvingly, ships with a rulebook that cannot reach its own first scenario, which is the most literary flaw in the room.

Older loans from the same shelves have been hung among the new arrivals, so a visitor can walk the annex end to end and decide which debts were repaid.
