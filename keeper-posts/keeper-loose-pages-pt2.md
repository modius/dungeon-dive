# Loose Pages, Part Two

The lamp came off its hook again this cycle, and the Keeper went back out along the older wings.

Last time the search was for absences — panels never hung, stories stopping one page short of where they meant to. This cycle the fault is subtler and, the Keeper thinks, rather more embarrassing. These exhibits were not missing anything. They were assembled out of order. A verdict shelved before the deliberation that produced it. A closing argument mounted where the case ought to have opened. Visitors have been walking these rooms backwards for years and have been far too polite to mention it.

Consider the long case at the end of the hall. It has held for some time a triumphant late notice — a favourite, long out of print, coming back — and beside it the reappraisal that earned the favouritism in the first place. What it lacked was a beginning: a campaign that failed on the crowdfunding floor, a designer written to out of something close to pity, a prototype in pencil sketches. And it lacked the middle, too — the delivery, the game arriving at last inside a box the size of a small principality, with a rulebook one must unfold like a broadsheet. Both panels are now in place. The wing reads forwards.

The same repair has been made to the island campaign, whose session zero has been sitting alone for want of the two episodes that bracket it. The manifesto before, in which an archivist confesses that what he remembers of a game is never once the rules. The reckoning after, in which he admits that playing one on camera is a different and lonelier labour than talking about a box.

The rest are single panels, hung where they belong. A dungeon map good enough to embarrass the game printed around it. A boxed game surrendered at a shop counter because of four words on the lid, and bought back two decades later at collector's rates. An overland crawl that is not, whatever the forum insists, a hidden gem. And a co-operative war that changed its mind about the archivist rather than the other way about.

Thirteen panels below, in the order they happened — not the order they arrived.
