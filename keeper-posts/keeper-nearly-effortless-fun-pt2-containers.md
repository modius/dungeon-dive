# The Low Door, Third Knock

The Keeper has stopped clearing space for these. The space is simply there now, kept free the way one keeps a chair free for a relative who visits without writing ahead.

Third crate. Same shelf, same low door, same lid that lifts without argument. *Nearly Effortless Fun* has become less a series than a standing arrangement.

What the Keeper noticed while shelving this consignment — and it took until the fifth exhibit to notice, which is embarrassing in a professional — is that these seven are quietly obsessed with **containers**. Not with adventures. With the things adventures are kept in.

One draws its dungeon out of a bag, a tile at a time, and believes that is the whole trick, and is right. One consists of a single deck doing four jobs at once — the monsters, the weapons, the loot, and the hero's own dwindling life — which is the most economical arrangement in this room by some margin. One arrived in the archivist's possession with no box whatsoever, having been shipped in a burlap sack, and he built it a house from the remains of a game he had stopped loving. One occasioned a formal ruling, delivered on camera with the gravity of a magistrate, on precisely how large a box may be before it ceases to be small. And one stands condemned — a perfectly decent little game sentenced to a lesser recommendation purely for the crime of arriving in a carton three sizes too grand, when a rival's spare box on the very same shelf would have fitted it exactly.

The Keeper finds this a deeply congenial preoccupation. Everything here is a container: this building, these shelves, the crates they came in. The archivist is, in his way, arguing the Keeper's own thesis back at him — that the vessel is never neutral, that a thing badly housed is a thing half-lost, and that the surest evidence somebody loves a game is that they went and made it a box.

The catalogue runs earliest to latest. The deeper low-friction stacks are folded in among the new arrivals, unmarked, as ever.
