# The Drafting Table

Most of this archive records games being *played*. This wing records them being *made* — which is a stranger business, and considerably less flattering to everyone involved.

The Keeper cleared a long table for it. On one end: books so beautiful they barely function as tools, bought by a man who freely admits he will never run them and reads them as weird fiction instead. In the middle: a designer and an archivist, meeting on a schedule across two years, talking about art packs and algorithms and how little either of them earns. At the far end, under better light than it deserves, the wreckage — a decade of the archivist's own abandoned games, laid out and labelled by cause of death.

That last stretch is why this exhibit exists. Anyone will show you a finished thing. Far fewer will show you the worker-placement dungeon crawl that collapsed under its own action economy, the five-tier nesting of world-environment-setting-area-location that ate itself, the d12 system that never balanced. Fewer still will name the actual disease: hitting a bad rule and *patching* it rather than going back, over and over, until what you have is not a game but a scaffold of repairs. The archivist names it, then spends the next diary demonstrating the cure on his own boss design — and the fix is so much better than the patch that the whole exhibit tilts toward optimism.

Between those poles sits the conversation itself, which keeps circling one idea: the **tail**. Games used to live for years and grow communities. Now they arrive complete and the talking stops on delivery. Both men would rather build something that outlasts its own announcement.

The catalogue below runs earliest to latest, some panels hung today and some hung seasons ago. The Keeper does not distinguish. Neither should you.
