# The Tavern Door, Part Two — D&D, the OSR, and the Fantasy Pub

The remaining crates came up off the landing, where they had sat since the first hanging, and the Keeper — which swept the step last cycle and then, characteristically, sat down on it — has given the rest of the wing over to a single habit of the archivist's. He keeps taking books apart.

Not unkindly. Look along the wall. Two zines bought on a Christmas morning and printed at home on card stock, one furnishing the inn down to the price of a bowl of lamb stew, the other supplying reasons to stay in it long past closing. A free game three books thick, of which the archivist recommends precisely one chapter — and recommends it chiefly to people who will never play the game that chapter belongs to. A handsome box of Vancian magic, opened with genuine love for its author and genuine honesty about its use, from which he means to scan four tables and pass the remainder along to somebody who will deserve it more. The Keeper, whose whole profession is the keeping of things whole, finds it has no objection whatever. A book that is looted is a book that was read.

Then the far side of the room, where the crawl itself gets re-pointed. A year-long megadungeon challenge cut down to one room a week and renamed accordingly, on the entirely sound reasoning that an unfinished dungeon is worse than a small one: a waterfall to dive beneath, a cult whose name is written but never voiced, purple robes refreshed nightly by nobody in particular. A tactical skirmish that opens with an explosion in a tavern basement and then declines, permanently, to ask whether you hit. And at the end of the row a city district in which the dungeon has been replaced by pubs — the same point crawl, the same travel times between rooms, the same clock running down over six days — except that the rooms serve drinks, and the thing being hunted has been mistaken for one and poured.

Five older panels hang among them, as they did in the first half, and the Keeper still declines to mark which. With this the wing is finished: the door hung, the step swept, the Keeper installed beside it in what it maintains is not a waiting posture. Merely the nearest available one.
