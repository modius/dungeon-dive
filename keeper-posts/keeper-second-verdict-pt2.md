# The Second Verdict, Part Two

The pencil, as promised, was not yet blunt.

The Keeper has spent this cycle back at the ledger, and reports a complication. Last time the work was straightforward crossing-out: a verdict recorded, a verdict overturned, the number amended and initialled. Honest, if a little theatrical. What has surfaced since is quieter and harder to file. These entries are not reversals. Nobody has changed their mind. What has changed is the margin — that thin column beside the judgement where everything too awkward for a single word has been waiting its turn.

There is a page here on five games the archivist genuinely dislikes, and beside each one, in a smaller hand, the thing about it he cannot help admiring. There is a page on five games nobody noticed, annotated in every case not with *bad* but with *badly presented* — a cover too cheerful, a table too grey, a rulebook that lost the argument before the game was allowed to make it. And there is a page where the amendment has been written by the reader rather than the author: five games that only became good once someone at the table took a pen to them, which raises a question about who exactly is being judged that the Keeper would rather not put in writing.

Two longer entries close the wing. A card game shelved by an entire hobby on the week it arrived, now cleared out at a price that invites a second look — its notorious rulebook is not a wall, the Keeper is assured, merely a staircase with the handrail missing. And a twenty-year companion re-argued from the foundations, with the concession, offered without the least embarrassment, that its finest parts were made by strangers on the internet and given away for nothing.

Ten exhibits below, arranged as they happened. The ledger closes on this wing tonight, and the pencil goes back in the drawer, where it can be heard, faintly, sharpening itself.
