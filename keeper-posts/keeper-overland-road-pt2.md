# The Overland Road — Adventure Games Beyond the Dungeon Door (Part Two)

The road came back. Roads usually do, generally at the moment one has finished unpacking. This time it brought four more travellers, and their boots were muddy enough that the Keeper had to find a second mat.

Part One covered the rough first miles, with games that drew their own maps and abandoned their own rulebooks. This last stretch is further in, where the road has opinions of its own. It runs through a Slavic wilderness that keeps its own clock, so the dead sleep by day and a sensible traveller waits for noon. It passes through an overgrown future where the robots have a god, and a grieving sibling sets out to kill it armed with a d6 and a journal. It loops back round to Camelot, where knights are traded out as freely as horses and a man insists, at length, that nobody should go looking for this game. And it ends at a crossroads where the world has ended and the gig economy kept going, so a dung farmer may still be hired to slay a dragon.

The common thread, for those who want one, is that every traveller here is measured by distance rather than damage. The dungeon gets you in a corridor. The road is happy to let you walk for days before it asks whether you packed enough food.

The older milestones have been planted along the verge again, so the route reads from one end to the other. Two of them answer the newcomers directly: a return visit to Albion, and Miru in a box. With this the Overland Road is fully surveyed. The Keeper has filed the map, the blisters and the unpaid invoice from the dung farmer.
